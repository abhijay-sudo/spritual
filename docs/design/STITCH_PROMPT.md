# Spiritual.co.in — Stitch Design Brief

## Your assignment
Design a distinctive, production-minded mobile-first experience for Spiritual.co.in, a teacher-led community practice companion for adults. This is a connected product, not a landing-page exercise or a generic meditation template.

The outcome and constraints below are binding; the proposed layouts are not a ceiling. Use your judgment to discover a clearer, more memorable experience. Prioritize useful originality, coherent interaction design, and careful refinement over decorative complexity.

## Product truth
An existing teacher or institution helps adults continue a trusted practice between weekly gatherings. The institution supplies and approves teaching; members follow short practices, read source context, and may reflect privately. AI is optional assistance, never the teacher, spiritual authority, or centerpiece.

The initial content model is a teacher-approved 14-day introduction leading into a 28-day practice cycle, with reusable recordings and four weekly explanations. One tradition and one language are selected for launch. Use English for this design exploration. Do not invent a launch tradition, sacred text, reviewer, credential, testimonial, or real partnership.

The institutional pilot has up to 30 adults. Institution-funded members must clearly see that access is included; never put a payment demand in front of an already-covered practice. Consumer subscriptions, gifts, public communities, and multi-tradition expansion are not launch features.

## Experience objective
Within the opening screen, a member should understand what to do today, why it belongs in their program, how long it takes, and whose teaching it is. Aim for a first useful action within 60 seconds; this is a usability hypothesis to test, not a result to claim.

Design for busy adults, different levels of digital confidence, readable text, one-handed interaction, and quiet everyday use. The experience should feel human, culturally respectful, warm, and trustworthy rather than mystical, clinical, addictive, or corporate.

## Creative exploration
Initially explore three genuinely different directions for the same Today screen and practice player. Change hierarchy, composition, and interaction emphasis, not just colors.

Suggested hypotheses, which you may improve:
A. Quiet Editorial: expressive typography, warm paper-like surfaces, disciplined composition.
B. Human Continuity: the weekly teacher-to-member relationship is visually central.
C. Tactile Simplicity: one clear practice, subtle depth, and exceptionally direct controls.

Recommend one coherent direction. Do not mash all three together. Explain the key tradeoff, select the strongest candidate, and continue designing the connected journey without waiting for a routine aesthetic decision.

If established brand assets are supplied, preserve them. Otherwise use a provisional typographic Spiritual wordmark, not an invented permanent sacred symbol.

## Visual direction
A promising starting palette is warm ivory, deep readable ink, forest/sage accents, and restrained brass highlights. This is a proposal, not a mandatory theme. Avoid a black-dominant interface, low-contrast beige text, generic purple gradients, excessive glass effects, and interchangeable rounded-card grids.

Consider an editorial display face paired with a highly legible interface face, using licensable fonts and no more than two families. Establish a coherent type scale, spacing scale, icon language, radii, borders, elevation, and semantic colors. Aim for at least 16px body text and comfortable approximately 44px touch targets; accommodate larger text and keyboard focus.

Do not decorate with randomly mixed religious symbols, fabricated scripture, AI-generated guru portraits, or claims of divine endorsement. Relevant original or licensed imagery is welcome when its provenance is recorded. The layout must remain excellent without stock photography.

## Connected journeys and screens
Design these as one system, with mobile states and suitable desktop adaptations:
1. Public entry and a complete try-without-registration sample, with a separate path for institutions.
2. Invitation acceptance: community identity, included access, expiry/error states, adult eligibility, and minimal preferences.
3. Today: one recommended approved practice; verified attribution; duration variants only when actually available; clear resume and gentle return-after-absence states.
4. Practice player: usable play/pause, seeking, transcript, source context, loading/interruption/recovery, optional completion, and download status.
5. My Program: a readable ordered journey, current position, published versus scheduled items, and honest progress without punitive streaks.
6. Private Reflection: optional writing, skip, save, export, and delete; plain local-device storage explanation. No default AI sharing or institution visibility.
7. Completion: quiet acknowledgment, one helpful next step, and no forced sharing, guilt, or spiritual score.
8. Account and Help: access source, access end date, reminders, accessibility, privacy, deletion, and applicable purchase/support controls.
9. Teacher console: the sequence from content preparation to rights verification, review, approval, scheduling, and publication. Include a minimal cohort/invitation view and an aggregate participation report.

My Circle, question submission, payments beyond the pilot, and AI interactions remain absent unless the implementation brief explicitly enables them. Do not add speculative navigation items.

## Motion and state design
Specify purposeful motion for navigation, player expansion, playback feedback, completion, progress updates, dialogs, and validation. Starting budgets: roughly 120–180ms for feedback and 180–280ms for transitions, adjustable after inspection.

Provide reduced-motion alternatives. Avoid scroll hijacking, forced intro sequences, flashing, autoplay sound, continuous battery-heavy animation, and effects that delay access. Do not introduce breathing techniques or pacing without approved practice content.

Cover loading, empty, unavailable, expired, offline, failed, success, and permission-denied states where relevant. For dialogs and sheets, specify focus entry/return, escape/back behavior, keyboard interaction, and mobile keyboard avoidance.

## Required handoff
Return the actual supported artifacts: selected designs, connected prototype where available, screenshots or screen references, design tokens, component/state inventory, responsive rules, and motion notes. Export DESIGN.md if supported; otherwise provide the equivalent rules for Codex to save locally.

Use believable but explicitly labeled demo content. Never imply a fixture is a real teacher approval or paid result. Preserve the chosen design language across every screen. Critique the result for visual sameness, excessive steps, weak hierarchy, privacy ambiguity, and implementation feasibility; fix concrete problems before handing it over.

Your design is a reference for implementation, not evidence that authentication, storage, billing, accessibility, or offline behavior already works.
