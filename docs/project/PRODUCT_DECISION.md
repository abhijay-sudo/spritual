## 1. The product thesis

**For a customer.** Every practice in Spiritual is written down before it is recorded. So while your teacher's voice reads the Sanskrit, you see three things moving in time with her: the line in its own script, the line spelled in English letters so you can say it, and one plain sentence telling you what it means. You can recite along, or read along in silence with the phone muted on a train, or take the same practice in 3, 5 or 10 minutes depending on the day you are actually having. Under every practice is the name of the person who recorded it, the name of the scholar who checked it, the exact source line it came from, and every correction ever made to it with the date. One complete practice a day is free forever. A year of the full program is $39 for you, $69 for your whole household of up to five, ₹2,499 in India — against Hallow and Calm at $69.99, and Hallow's family plan at $119.99.

**Why incumbents cannot follow — and the honest boundary of that claim.** Three locks, in descending order of how hard they bind, and I am naming what each one does *not* cover.

1. **Catalogue-inverse credibility (strongest, and named by the competitor).** A named reviewer and a dated per-line correction log on every asset is a claim whose credibility *falls* as catalogue size rises. Hallow's own head of product, judging the trust thesis, wrote it himself: *"I can align and gloss my catalogue in a quarter; I cannot put a named human's signature and a per-line correction history on ten thousand assets at any price, and if I published a correction log at my scale it would read as a defect register."* Insight Timer is worse off: it holds 300,000+ tracks under a licence its own Teacher Terms state is **non-exclusive** with *"You retain full ownership"* — it structurally cannot re-author its catalogue at all, and its users already report obvious AI content sitting unremoved for weeks (Leanne Edwards, Trustpilot, Jul 2026). We have 42 assets and one named scholar. This is not "we care more." It is an arithmetic property of catalogue size.

2. **The corpus nobody with an editorial apparatus is in.** Line-level meaning on non-Hindi Indian-language devotional text. Hallow is not in this market — and for its own liturgical corpus the Hallow judge is right that the script pre-exists the audio, so it could ship this for Latin and scripture in a quarter. Sri Mandir *is* in this market, and carries verified quality failures on exactly this axis (App Store: *"Pronunciation of names are mostly wrong"*; incorrect gotra) while spending ₹51.9 crore of advertising against ₹69.6 crore of revenue for a ₹45.3 crore net loss — it cannot fund an editorial apparatus that does not feed booking volume. Astrotalk's entire ₹1,176 crore is open-ended per-minute wallet spend; a flat capped price is that revenue model cancelled. Sadhguru's Miracle of Mind ships ~11 Indian languages **free** and beats us on price absolutely — but it is one teacher's tradition and it is meditation, not scripture-with-meaning.

3. **Teacher audience portability (real, but supply-side only).** Insight Timer's own help centre bans teachers referencing *"outside apps, websites, workshops, books, courses hosted elsewhere, social media accounts, retreats, email newsletters,"* enforced *"everywhere on Insight Timer"* with profile closure, and their stated rationale is that permitting it *"would undermine the ability to invest in the platform."* They then paid a **$550,000 Teacher Assistance Fund** to teachers their August 2024 re-cut harmed. A one-click roster export is the thing they have written down that they cannot offer. **But this protects teacher supply, not user demand** — the Hallow judge was right that no end customer ever chose an app because their teacher could export them. It is a recruiting term, priced at 2 build-days, and it does not appear in any customer-facing claim.

**Not a moat, stated so nobody double-counts it:** the one-tap cancel button, the pre-charge reminder email, the published refund SLA, the accessibility nutrition label (Apple states labels become *required* over time — that gap closes on Apple's published schedule whether we exist or not), the payment rail (worth $0.31–$4.06 per payer-year), and "no per-user TTS" (a strawman — Hallow, Calm and Glorify all ship pre-recorded human narration; the cost-structure unit economist was right to call this the one genuinely misleading line in that thesis). These are table stakes and cost lines. They are not defensibility.

**Total identified: roughly $14–$23 of a $31 price gap. The remainder is not funded — it is forgone.** Here is the sentence no thesis wrote:

> **The blueprint reaches ~$10k/month net profit at 2,800 payers on $9 ARPPU *plus* 224 packs/month *plus* 20 institutions. This plan ships none of those. Subscription-only at $21.19/account, $10k/month net needs 8,700–14,000 accounts on a lean $2,000/mo fixed base, or 11,200–18,000 on the blueprint's $6,500 base.** That is 3–6× the blueprint's payer count, from a distribution channel with no measured size. That is the true price of the discount, and it is the single largest commercial risk in this document.

**Breakeven, which is the number that actually matters first:**

| fixed base | CAC $0 | CAC $4 | CAC $8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lean $2,000/mo | **1,133 accounts** | 1,396 | 1,820 |
| Blueprint $6,500/mo | 3,681 | 4,538 | 5,914 |

**Content spend must be staged, and this is a build-order decision:** at 300 accounts a $10k content bill is $33.33/account and year one is **−$12.14**. It turns positive at ~500 accounts. So record the 14-day introduction first (~$3,500), and do not commission the 28-day cycle until 500 accounts are paying.

### Three unpriced holes, named not buried

1. **Foreign consumption tax.** UK/EU B2C digital VAT registration thresholds for a non-established supplier are commonly **nil**, and EU/UK law requires consumer prices displayed VAT-inclusive. I have modelled a 5% blended drag at a 30% UK/EU mix. At 45% it is 7.5% and eats 72% of the entire rail saving. **A chartered accountant answers this before the first overseas rupee**, per CORRECTIONS open question #3.
2. **The 3% refund provision is invented.** No incumbent's refund-grant rate exists in any source. At 10% it costs 15% of contribution.
3. **Content production has no quote.** $10–15k is ASSUMED and it decides year-one viability.

## 5. What we deliberately do NOT build

Each of these makes the product better, not merely cheaper. Where it only makes it cheaper, I say so.

1. **No AI chat companion, no synthetic voice in any practice.** *Better:* the verified retention datum runs the wrong way for AI apps (21.1% vs 30.7% annual — with the corpus's own warning that this is not causal). Insight Timer's AI policy already requires human narration and bans voice clones, which is a competitor giving up cheap supply to hold a line. And synthesised sacred-language audio is the exact axis users already complain about. *Cheaper too:* the schema constraint in feature 1 makes per-user generation impossible by construction.
2. **No streaks, no badges, no leaderboards.** *Better:* the streak reset is the moment the habit dies. Evidence is weak but real (research_competitors.md L85), and the design follows from the lapse-aware re-entry screen either way.
3. **No wallet, no per-minute meter, no stored balance, no top-up, no donate-then-auto-renew.** *Better:* price certainty stated as a product promise. Structurally immune to the entire Astrotalk (55% one-star, rating suppressed for a guidelines breach) and Pray.com complaint class. *Costs Astrotalk ~100% of its revenue model to match — but funds nothing for us, so it is not in the funding table.*
4. **No ads at any tier.** *Better:* an ad between a person and an act of worship is a cultural-fit failure, not an annoyance. Pray.com cannot match this: the $7 gap between $7.99 Premium and $14.99 ad-free *is* the ad revenue.
5. **No daily new content.** *Better:* 42 deeply-reviewed assets with named reviewers and correction logs beat a treadmill nobody finishes. *Cheaper:* Glorify's audited cost of sales at 2.85× revenue is what the treadmill costs. **I am not pretending this is only a user benefit — it is primarily a cost decision, and a buyer comparing "42" to "thousands" will feel it as a downgrade.** See R3.
6. **No astrology, no puja/chadhava bookings, no temple fulfilment.** *Better:* the failure mode is an unverifiable physical service; Sri Mandir's complaint class is non-delivery and ₹45.3 crore of net loss. Already excluded at blueprint L212.
7. **No public feed, no comments, no social discovery, no DMs.** *Better:* a devotional product with a public feed is a moderation business. The only social surface is a named circle with a shared start date and a teacher who can see two fields.
8. **No individual India SKU.** *Better:* it forces the household framing that matches actual Indian usage. *Honest:* it exists because every India individual price is contribution-negative on our own cost stack.
9. **No native app at launch.** *Better:* ships weeks sooner and avoids Play's 14-day/12-tester gate plus ~1 week review. *Cheaper:* $4.06/payer-year, the $99 Apple fee, the $25 Play fee, RevenueCat's 1% of **all** tracked revenue above $2,500 MTR.
10. **No free tier with AI.** *Derived, not preferred:* a free tier carrying per-user inference costs $17.16/payer/year — 59% of a $29 price and 44% of $39. The free tier is one complete daily practice from the cached library, and it is genuinely complete: full three-column view, full transcript, silent mode. **The devotional act is free; what is invoiced is the teaching** — a named teacher's curriculum, translation, review and correction history, which is labour a person actually performed. That is the only honest answer to the live objection that attaches to monetising devotion itself (Crisis Magazine on *"monetization and celebritization of prayer"*; Giles Fraser comparing $69.99 to indulgences; *"It's a scam that uses God's name"*; *"Why would you make people pay for something Allah will reward you for"*).
11. **No retraction, ever.** Anything free or included when an account signs up stays that way for that account, in writing. *Cheap for us because we have no free tier to retract; expensive for Glorify and Insight Timer, who have already spent that credibility* (LittleMike23's 1-star is about Listen and Daily Walk having been free; Insight Timer's "Angel of light" about a withdrawn grandfathered code).

## 6. Week one, concretely

**Meera, 34, Toronto. Grew up saying a Sanskrit stotra with her grandmother. Can say every syllable. Has never known what any of it means. Her aunt's teacher in Chennai sent a WhatsApp message on Saturday: "Our circle starts Monday. 14 seats. Link below."**

### Day 1, Monday

**08:40 — the link.** Mobile web, no app install, no account wall. The page shows the teacher's name and face, the tradition, the start date (today), seats remaining, and — before any signup — a 40-second sample of the actual first practice, playing with all three columns moving. She watches the Devanagari highlight, the English spelling underneath, and a short sentence appear: *"I bow to the one who removes what obstructs."* She has said that line for twenty-five years.

**08:43 — the free practice.** She taps "do today's practice" without signing up. It asks one question: *how much time do you have right now?* She taps 3 minutes. The practice runs. The lines move. She reads along. At the end: one screen showing what she just did, the name of the teacher who recorded it, the name of the scholar who checked it, the source (text, edition, verse), and "revision 2 — see what changed."

**08:47 — the paywall she doesn't hit.** There isn't one. The screen says: today's practice is free, every day, forever. The 42-day program with the circle is $39 for you or $69 for your household of five. Underneath, in the same size type: *we will email you seven days before we charge you again, with a one-tap link to stop it.* And beside the annual plan, a second option: *prepaid year — does not auto-renew.*

**09:05 — she buys the household.** Cashfree/Razorpay checkout. She enters her own email, then three more: her mother in Chennai, her husband, her aunt. Each invite shows **pending** until accepted. The confirmation email carries a reference number, the exact renewal date, and the cancel link.

**19:30 — her mother joins.** Her mother had an individual plan on a different app and asks whether she has to cancel it. Not here — there is no individual plan to upgrade from, and if there had been, the unused remainder would have been credited, not forfeited. Her mother's practice history and reflections are private from Meera by construction. Meera cannot see them and there is no setting that would let her.

**21:10 — silent mode.** Baby asleep in the next room. She opens Day 1 again, taps the speaker icon off. Same practice, no sound. The lines advance on their own timing with a small haptic tick at each transition. She finishes it in silence at full volume of meaning.

### Days 2–7

**Day 2 (Tue).** Today opens on Day 2 — the same day her mother, her aunt and eleven other people are on. She takes the 5-minute variant this time; it is the same recording with two optional segments included, not a different, worse one. Afterwards a text box: *anything you want to note?* She writes two lines. A note under the box says the reflection stays on this device unless she chooses to send it.

**Day 3 (Wed).** Busy. 3-minute variant at 22:50. Done.

**Days 4–5 (Thu–Fri).** Missed. Both days. Nothing happens. No notification saying she broke anything. No red number.

**Day 6 (Sat).** She opens the app at 07:00 expecting to feel bad. Today says: *Your circle is on Day 6. Here is Day 6 — or here is the 3-minute way back in.* There is no backlog offered, no streak shown as broken, no zero. She takes the 3-minute re-entry, then does Day 6 anyway.

**Day 7 (Sun).** A WhatsApp message from the teacher — not the app, the teacher, from a platform number, on the channel she already uses: *"Meera, saw you'd gone quiet mid-week. The Thursday line is the one everyone finds hard. Ask me on the call if you want."* Meera had appeared on a list showing the teacher exactly two fields: her name, and the date of her last completed practice. Nothing else. Not what she wrote. Not how long she practised. She had opted into that list with a separate tap at join, and can leave it with one tap at no cost.

**What she does not experience in week one:** a trial converting to a charge she did not expect. An upsell inside a practice. An ad. A wallet asking to be topped up. A request to rate the app. A streak. A push notification at a time she did not choose. A feature she cannot use because she is on the free tier and it is Sunday.

## 7. The honest risks, ranked

**R1 — The comprehension demand is unevidenced, and the project's own researcher warned specifically against building on it.** Verbatim: *"Do not build on this yet. It is the single most attractive-sounding thesis in the whole set and the one with the weakest evidence — which is exactly the combination that produces a wrong product."* No first-person quote, no app review, no forum post says "I don't understand the mantras" — only third-party commentary and the Pluralism Project's evidence that temples spend real money on the problem.

*Why I am building it anyway, and it is one reason only:* **the three-column view is not a bet on comprehension.** It is a rendering of the script record we build regardless, because the same record produces the captions, the silent mode, the length variants and language parity at full cost. If comprehension does not sell, we have lost the 5 days of renderer (feature 2), not the product. That asymmetry is the whole argument. *Mitigation, from the research's own prescription:* before the 28-day cycle is commissioned, sell a paid 14-day bilingual practice-with-meaning program to one temple's mailing list and count buyers. Adjacent verified evidence exists (Sri Mandir's pronunciation and name-handling complaints) but it is adjacent, not identical.

**R2 — We need 3–6× the blueprint's payer count from a channel with no measured size.** $21.19/account against a $10k-net target of 8,700–18,000 accounts. Teacher-sourced acquisition is the entire plan and it is unproven; paid media is excluded because excluding it is what funds the price. Sri Mandir's ₹51.9 crore of advertising against ₹69.6 crore of revenue is what the alternative costs. **Mitigation:** breakeven on a lean $2,000/month base is 1,133–1,820 accounts, which is a reachable first target and the only number to manage toward for twelve months. Do not staff to the $6,500 base until 3,700 accounts exist.

**R3 — At the moment of purchase this looks like a downgrade.** Every customer judge in the dossier landed here. Forty-two assets against thousands, for $2.50/month saved. The "eight favourite tracks by week four" defence describes consumption, not choice, and is the weakest-sourced claim we hold; people knowingly buy optionality they never use. **Mitigation:** the three-column view and silent mode are visible in a single screenshot and a six-second preview; the cohort deadline is the conversion device; and the household — where the saving is $51–71/year, not $31 — is the SKU to lead with. **Accept that we do not win a satisfied Calm subscriber. The buyer is someone a teacher personally asked.**

**R4 — Hallow can neutralise two of three user-visible differentiators in one quarter.** Their own head of product said how: liturgical scripts pre-exist the audio; word-level forced alignment (WhisperX/MFA) costs cents per audio hour; an LLM gloss-and-translation pass with human spot-QA runs "well under $50k inside one quarter"; and their 1/3/5/10/15-minute picker already ships. **Mitigation:** the two that survive are (a) the named-reviewer-plus-correction-log, whose credibility scales inversely with catalogue size — stated by that same judge as uncopyable at his scale — and (b) a corpus Hallow is not in. So the language and tradition choice is load-bearing, and the selection rule is: **one non-Hindi Indian language, one tradition, where a credible named reviewer and one teacher with a 200+ roster both say yes.** Do not pick it from search volume.

**R5 — "The teacher was told you stopped praying" is a screenshot, not a feature.** Article 9 special-category data, a WhatsApp message triggered by absence from devotional practice, in the one category where surveillance framing is fatal. No competitor has to do anything to make this happen. **Mitigation:** separate affirmative opt-in at join, revocable in one tap at zero cost, two fields only, platform number never the teacher's personal number, and the consent copy written by whoever would have to defend it publicly. If it lands wrong once, it is the product's name forever.

**R6 — The India leg is thin enough that one wrong assumption makes it negative.** ₹2,499/4 seats contributes $3.33–$6.85 and requires all three of India-staffed support, a zero-inference free tier, and household-only pricing. The Cashfree 0% domestic window expires **31 March 2027**. And against Miracle of Mind's ~11 free Indian languages, **nothing here beats free** — the buyer's question becomes "why pay at all," which is the harder question.

**R7 — Three unpriced or invented numbers.** Foreign consumption tax (CANNOT_VERIFY, nil thresholds likely, needs a CA before the first overseas rupee). The 3% refund provision (invented — no incumbent's refund rate exists anywhere). Content production cost (no quote; decides year-one viability and inverts below ~500 accounts).

**R8 — Launch-blocking operational traps.** Supabase SMTP (2 msg/hour to team addresses only; 30/hour even after custom SMTP) silently kills every signup and every pre-charge email. Razorpay international activation has **no published SLA** and is the true critical path — file day 0. Vercel Hobby's fair-use guidelines name payment processing as the first example of prohibited commercial use — put checkout on Cloudflare Pages/Workers. Play's 14-day/12-tester gate is avoidable entirely by registering as an **organisation** rather than a personal account; decide this week.

### The three tests that should run before this build finishes, in priority order

1. **One teacher, one dated cohort, 30 seats at $39/$69, sold through their existing WhatsApp roster.** Measure: how many pay, and how many complete a practice in the first 48 hours. This is the acquisition question, and nothing in the document matters as much.
2. **A VoiceOver user and a TalkBack user on five incumbents, 20 minutes each, recorded.** Converts the strongest accessibility claim from REPORTED to VERIFIED and produces demo footage. AppleVis returned HTTP 403 — until this runs, the Insight Timer unlabeled-timer-button claim appears in **nothing public.**
3. **A chartered accountant on UK/EU VAT and GST-on-export.** Largest unpriced item in the model.

Ten teacher interviews on the roster-export question remain open and unresolved — no teacher has been found asking for it in their own words. It costs 2 build-days, so it ships regardless, but it carries no weight in the plan until those interviews happen.
