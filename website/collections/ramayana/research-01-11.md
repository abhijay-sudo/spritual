# Ramayana collection — chapters 1–11 editorial research

Research date: **26 October 2026**. Status: **original local editorial drafts; human review pending**.

These notes accompany `chapters-01-11.mjs`. They are not a publication clearance, a commercial licence, or an approval by a named religious scholar. The original public retellings and their recorded approvals were not changed. Those approvals do not transfer to these new words, the expanded episode boundaries, Hindi adaptations, or new artwork.

## What was actually done

- Wrote 11 connected English/Hindi chapters, with four scenes per chapter. The narrative passes from the poem’s opening frame through Hanuman taking the brothers to Sugriva. The second half is a separate file owned by another writer.
- Inspected the listed online Valmiki Ramayana Sanskrit/romanised text pages, verse glosses, and introductions using the web tool. Particular verse passages were inspected for the withdrawal of astras, Shabari’s food, Hanuman’s speech, and Rama’s grief at Dasharatha’s death.
- Used ancient narrative events to compose new prose. No passage of a modern translation is reproduced as a quotation, and the Hindi is a fresh adaptation of this draft rather than a copied published Hindi translation.
- Kept narrative and interpretation distinguishable. Interior thoughts, direct conversations and miracles not supported by the selected episode were not invented. Transitional language and reflections are the edition’s own editorial voice.
- Source coverage is episode-level checking, not a full critical-edition collation, manuscript study, or independent Sanskrit proofread. The linked site includes both verses and later explanatory commentary; commentary and its invented “Annex” dialogues must not be mistaken for the ancient narrative.
- The book is a selected, abridged journey. It is not every sarga of the Ramayana, and the larger collection’s stopping point at the return does not include Uttara Kanda.

## Rights and publication boundary

The underlying ancient narrative is distinct from a modern website’s translation, commentary, photography, and edition design. The links establish where episode checks occurred; they do not grant permission to copy those modern materials. No web images, modern translation blocks, audio, fonts, or scans have been imported into these two files. Do not label the content “rights cleared” solely because it links to an old scripture or because automated comparison passes.

Before publication or paid use, a human reviewer needs to approve the **exact bilingual version** and its source mapping. A rights reviewer must record the basis for publishing this original adaptation in the intended markets, and separately clear each illustration and voice. Review of generated art needs both provenance and iconographic judgment. Corrections change the reviewed version and require a new approval record. The engine should remain fail-closed while those records are absent.

## 01 — The story begins

Checked sources:

- [Bala 1.1](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga1/balaroman1.htm): Valmiki’s qualities/questions, Narada identifying Rama, and the account’s overview.
- [Bala 1.2](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga2/balaroman2.htm): Tamasa, Bharadvaja, the krauncha pair, the killing and verse, Brahma’s commission.
- [Bala 1.4](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga4/balaroman4.htm): teaching Kusha and Lava; performance; Rama as listener.

Checked beats: the opening is framed as inquiry, not a fabricated personal encounter; the killed bird is male; grief leads to the metrical utterance; composition and recitation follow. No direct quotation of the well-known first verse is reproduced.

Human review: approve expansion from the earlier narrow 1.1 reading into 1.2 and 1.4; approve the framing of Rama as both subject and listener. The editorial connection between compassion and storytelling is an interpretation. Avoid claiming the opening supplies independent historical proof. Art must not depict Valmiki already writing the entire epic while Narada is still only answering unless it is clearly an artistic composite.

## 02 — The princes and the teacher

ID: `princes-and-teacher`.

Checked sources:

- [Bala 1.18](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga18/balaroman18.htm): births, family relationships, upbringing and Vishvamitra’s arrival.
- [Bala 1.20](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga20/balaroman20.htm): Dasharatha’s objections and offer to go with his army.
- [Bala 1.22](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga22/balaroman22.htm): consent after Vasishtha’s counsel, blessings, departure, Bala and Atibala.
- [Bala 1.26](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga26/balaroman26.htm): Tataka’s assault, Rama’s initial restraint, ensuing death.
- [Bala 1.27](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga27/balaroman27.htm): overnight stay followed by the gift of astras.

Checked beats: mothers and sons are correctly paired; Lakshmana/Rama and Shatrughna/Bharata relationships; Dasharatha’s reluctance is not erased; killing is not made into a consequence-free training game. The draft gives no precise age for the princes because readings and later reckonings vary.

Human review: check terms `Bala`, `Atibala`, `astra` and their Hindi spellings; assess non-graphic handling of Tataka and the interpretation of a teacher’s command. This episode must not be used to teach unquestioning obedience to real-world harmful orders. An illustration should distinguish the brothers from the adult sage and avoid portraying the young princes as modern child soldiers or claiming documentary realism.

## 03 — Knowing how to stop

ID: `knowing-how-to-stop`.

Checked sources:

- [Bala 1.27](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga27/balaroman27.htm): weapon transmission and personified powers.
- [Bala 1.28](https://valmikiramayan.net/baala/sarga28/balasans28.htm): especially 1.28.2–3, Rama requesting *samhara* and Vishvamitra answering; the forest inquiry at the end.

Checked beats: the request follows receiving astras; it is made by Rama; the teacher grants further knowledge. “Withdrawal or counteraction” is used instead of claiming that every weapon is physically recalled in exactly the same way. The comparative calm of the forest transition is the editor’s reading, not a Sanskrit quotation.

Retrieval caveat: the non-`www` Sanskrit URL opened successfully on the initial web check; later attempts to revisit its lower lines timed out. A terminal follow-up failed local certificate validation; verification was not disabled. Reconfirm the whole chapter against a trusted edition during human review.

Human review: Sanskrit competence is especially useful for *samhara/upasamhara* terminology. Keep the narrative short rather than adding a made-up battle or a magical “practice scene.” Any animation of abstract energy is illustration, not literal source evidence.

## 04 — The road to Mithila

ID: `road-to-mithila`.

Checked sources:

- [Bala 1.30](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga30/balasans30.htm): six-day/six-night guard, Maricha driven away alive, Subahu killed, rite completed.
- [Bala 1.31](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga31/balaroman31.htm): journey to Janaka’s rite and invitation to see the bow.
- [Bala 1.43](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga43/balaroman43.htm): Bhagiratha, Ganga and Shiva in the embedded narrative.
- [Bala 1.49](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga49/balaroman49.htm): Ahalya becoming visible, greeting the princes, Gautama returning.
- [Bala 1.66](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga66/balaroman66.htm): invitation and the bow’s link to Sita’s marriage.

Checked beats: the sacrifice concludes before the Mithila journey; Maricha survives (important for chapter 9); the Ganga story is explicitly told within the journey. Ahalya is not represented as a stone, because that is not the selected Valmiki episode’s account.

Human review: Ahalya’s preceding story of disguise, agency and punishment is highly sensitive and varies by telling. The chapter deliberately summarises the restoration without deciding all questions of consent or declaring the punishment just. Review whether the adult/family framing needs a separate contextual note. The broader journey contains many stories omitted here; do not market this chapter as a complete account of Bala 1.29–66.

## 05 — The bow at Mithila

ID: `the-bow-at-mithila`.

Checked sources:

- [Bala 1.66](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga66/balaroman66.htm): Sita’s finding in the furrow and Janaka’s marriage condition.
- [Bala 1.67](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga67/balaroman67.htm): wheeled chest, permission, lifting/stringing/breaking, sound, sending messengers.
- [Bala 1.73](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga73/balaroman73.htm): weddings and their pairings.

Checked beats: the bow breaks while Rama strings/draws it, rather than because he sets out to smash a sacred object; permission precedes handling; the marriages are not declared complete at the instant the bow breaks. Pairings: Rama/Sita, Lakshmana/Urmila, Bharata/Mandavi, Shatrughna/Shrutakirti.

Human review: approve the wider boundary including the marriages. No garden romance or private dialogue has been invented. The local edition jumps over the separate Parashurama encounter and return journey into the succession episode; that omission is stated in the chapter note. Art should not imply a simultaneous stage full of rival suitors or Sita’s exact position in the bow hall unless the chosen source supports it. This is Janaka’s condition in the narrative, not a recommended model of contemporary marriage.

## 06 — The crown and the forest

ID: `promise-of-exile`.

Checked sources:

- [Ayodhya 2.18](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/ayodhya/sarga18/ayodhyaroman18.htm): Kaikeyi’s explanation, the boons and Dasharatha’s distress.
- [Ayodhya 2.30](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/ayodhya/sarga30/ayodhyaroman30.htm): Sita’s forceful insistence and Rama’s agreement.
- [Ayodhya 2.40](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/ayodhya/sarga40/ayodhyaroman40.htm): preparations complete, farewells, chariot, city and family grief.

Checked beats: Bharata and Shatrughna are away; two demands are Bharata’s installation and fourteen years’ exile; Rama initially asks Sita to stay; her eventual accompaniment is her argued choice, not silent obedience. Lakshmana is a willing companion.

Human review: the early succession and Manthara setup is condensed across 2.1–17; collate that bridge against a selected print/critical edition. Confirm the wording of Bharata’s proposed status (“installed”/“अभिषेक”) and avoid claims about exact ages or years since marriage. Rama’s acceptance must not erase the distress recorded by the poem or be promoted as an instruction for tolerating abusive family demands today.

## 07 — A friend beside the Ganga

ID: `a-friend-beside-the-ganga`.

Checked sources:

- [Ayodhya 2.50](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/ayodhya/sarga50/ayodhyaroman50.htm): farewell to Kosala, Shringaverapura, Guha, hospitality and horses.
- [Ayodhya 2.51](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/ayodhya/sarga51/ayodhyaroman51.htm): Lakshmana and Guha’s night watch and concern for family.
- [Ayodhya 2.52](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/ayodhya/sarga52/ayodhyaroman52.htm): boat, Sumantra’s departure, matted hair and Sita’s prayer.

Checked beats: Guha is a friend and Nishada chief, not an anonymous paid ferryman; the travellers’ chosen discipline shapes which hospitality they accept; the horses still receive care; the chariot does not continue across with them. The practical help is followed by an actual crossing.

Human review: keep Guha’s dignity and agency clear in both languages. Do not insert foot washing, a magical boat, or an exchange of ferryman’s fees from another retelling into this sequence. Review illustration props and avoid portraying the boat as a modern launch or the river crossing as a paid ritual service.

The omission is disclosed quietly in the note rather than turned into a teaser. Images must show foot-shaped wooden padukas, not modern sandals or a pair of generic shoes. Keep symbolic authority distinct from a claim that a physical object makes decisions.

ID: `forest-torn-apart`.

- [Aranya 3.30](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga30/aranyaroman30.htm): Khara’s defeat.
- [Aranya 3.42](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga42/aranyaroman42.htm): Maricha’s reluctance and the deer disguise.
- [Aranya 3.45](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga45/aranyaroman45.htm): the mimicked cry, Sita/Lakshmana dispute and his departure.
- [Aranya 3.49](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga49/aranyaroman49.htm), [3.50](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga50/aranyaroman50.htm), [3.51](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga51/aranyaroman51.htm): abduction and Jatayu’s intervention.
- [Aranya 3.68](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/aranya/sarga68/aranyaroman68.htm): Jatayu’s testimony, death and funeral.

Checked beats: Maricha survives the earlier rite attack, so his later return is coherent; he is not an ordinary deer accidentally killed; the cry is a deception, not proof of Rama being wounded; Sita refuses Ravana; Jatayu fights after counsel fails; Rama performs rites before resuming the search.

Human review: this condenses a broad and difficult passage. Check the missing intermediate connective details (3.31–41, 3.43–44, 3.46–48 and 3.52–67) against the chosen edition. No Lakshmana-rekha is added. Do not blame Sita for being abducted or romanticise Ravana’s violence. Shurpanakha’s disfigurement is named, not celebrated; chapter art must remain non-graphic. Geographic direction should stay broad without unsupported GPS coordinates or modern historical certainty.

Human review: Kabandha’s earlier encounter and release at 3.71–72 are only referenced, not fully retold. Review the restrained treatment of Shabari’s departure: the source’s fire imagery is deliberately not described as an actionable procedure. Do not import the website’s “Annex” dialogue, its later verse quotations, or assertions about caste and gender as if they were spoken in this scene. Check the reception/fruit wording against Sanskrit and approve any art showing specific fruit species; the source here does not specify a mandatory berry variety.

## 11 — The meeting at Rishyamuka

ID: `hanumans-first-conversation`.

Checked sources:

- [Kishkindha 4.2](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/kish/sarga2/kishkindharoman2.htm): Sugriva’s fear and sending Hanuman.
- [Kishkindha 4.3](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/kish/sarga3/kishkindharoman3.htm): assumed ascetic form, questions, identity, Rama’s comments on speech (especially 4.3.28–33).
- [Kishkindha 4.4](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/kish/sarga4/kishkindharoman4.htm): Lakshmana’s account, mutual possibility, dropping the disguise and carrying the brothers towards Sugriva.

Checked beats: Sugriva is not already a trusting ally; Hanuman’s approach precedes the agreement; language is the expressly praised skill; the encounter ends in movement towards the next meeting. No invented spoken lines, instant telepathic recognition or unsupported meeting rituals are inserted.

Human review: retain distinction between narrator’s interpretation of trust and the verses’ description of speech. An artwork depicting Hanuman in his familiar vanara form can illustrate the later part of the chapter; it must not be captioned as a literal depiction of the disguised first approach. The next chapter owns the alliance and Vali episode, avoiding duplicate events.
