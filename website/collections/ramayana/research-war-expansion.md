## Scope and sequence

The former single war chapter is now three connected chapters, each with four bilingual scenes. The account remains an abridgement of selected Yuddha Kanda episodes, not a verse-complete translation or an account of every battle. It ends the war at the accession of Vibhishana and the instruction to carry news to Sita; the existing next chapter handles her response and reunion.

Four recovery events must remain distinct:

1. **6.45 and 6.50:** Indrajit’s serpent-arrows bind the brothers. Garuda arrives; the serpents flee and the brothers recover. Hanuman does not complete a mountain mission here.
2. **6.74:** After a later Indrajit attack associated with Brahma’s weapon, Jambavan directs Hanuman’s first herb mission. The herbs hide from view; Hanuman brings the summit; their fragrance revives the brothers and allied vanaras.
3. **6.90–91:** Lakshmana kills Indrajit and returns wounded, supported by Hanuman and Vibhishana. Sushena treats him and the other wounded. This is not the later spear injury.
4. **6.100–101:** Ravana attacks Vibhishana; Lakshmana intervenes and is himself struck by Ravana’s later spear. Sushena establishes that Lakshmana is alive and directs a separate mountain mission. Hanuman cannot identify the herbs, brings a summit again, and Sushena prepares/administers the medicine. Lakshmana recovers and speaks to Rama.

## Variants, omissions and human-review items

- **Named source/editorial reviewer:** confirm the exact sequence above against the chosen Sanskrit edition, including the weapon episodes, Nikumbhila and the two mountain journeys. This pass consulted the linked text/translation, not a full manuscript or critical-edition apparatus. No named expert has approved the new version.
- **Hindi reviewer:** review these exact translations for literary flow, equivalence, honorific consistency and names such as निकुम्भिला, कुम्भकर्ण, जाम्बवान and आदित्यहृदय. Hindi is original draft prose, not a licensed Hindi translation. Automated bilingual presence checks do not establish translation quality.
- **Rights/provenance reviewer:** distinguish the ancient narrative from the modern website’s copyrighted expression. No licence to reuse its translation, annotations, source-site images or audio is asserted. These drafts must receive their own exact-version rights decision before commercial publication.
- **Devotional and cultural review:** the sun hymn and extraordinary recoveries remain within the epic’s devotional narrative. There is no promise that recitation or herbs produce modern medical outcomes. No plant identification, dosage or self-treatment instruction is supplied. Do not market these passages as healing evidence.
- **Family reading and violence review:** non-graphic narration still includes death, bereavement, serious injury and fear for a sibling’s life. Content notes remain present. No age rating, child-suitability certification or participant research is implied.
- **No silent tradition blending:** no Kalanemi encounter, Bharata shooting Hanuman, sun being stopped, pre-dawn deadline, nectar in Ravana’s navel, or other popular elaboration is inserted into these scenes. Such episodes would need their own named source/tradition and reviewed treatment. No single geographical identification of the herb mountain is asserted.
- **Avoid misleading kinship or moral simplification:** Angada remains Vali’s son. The source site has occasional summary/translation slips; they are not imported mechanically. Vibhishana’s disagreement with his nephew is indirect narration, not an endorsement of unrestricted betrayal or unconditional family obedience. Mandodari can grieve and condemn the abduction in the same scene. Sita is never blamed for Ravana’s actions.
- **Artwork remains separate:** imagery for Angada, Garuda, Kumbhakarna, Nikumbhila, the two recoveries, Matali and Mandodari needs scene-level iconographic/editorial review. Do not describe a reused battle painting as if it depicts an absent character or specific medical action. The text file’s art fallback does not grant art approval.
