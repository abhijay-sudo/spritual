# Generated collection art

These original editorial images were generated for Spritual’s collection cards and converted to responsive WebP pairs. They are decorative orientation art, not depictions claimed to be canonical scenes.

## Mahabharata

Files: `collection-mahabharata.webp`, `collection-mahabharata-sm.webp`

Prompt direction: a premium vertical editorial landscape at dawn, an ancient Indian crossroads and distant encampments, a weathered chariot wheel and cloth in the foreground, manuscript-like botanical linework, forest green, ochre and warm ivory, no named figures, no text, no logos, contemplative rather than spectacular.

## Ramayana

Files: `collection-ramayana.webp`, `collection-ramayana-sm.webp`

Prompt direction: a premium vertical editorial river-and-forest landscape at dawn, a distant hill city, an unoccupied bow and quiver resting beside the path, warm ivory, forest green and muted copper, no named figures, no text, no logos, culturally respectful and calm.

## Shiva sources

Files: `collection-shiva.webp`, `collection-shiva-sm.webp`

Prompt direction: a premium vertical Himalayan landscape at dawn, a distant stone shrine, river and crescent moon, an unoccupied trident and copper vessel in the foreground, indigo, stone, forest green and brass light, no deity portrait, no text, no logos, contemplative and source-oriented.

The full images are 1120 × 1400; the small variants are 560 × 700. On 1 October 2026, the project owner explicitly attested that they had reviewed and approved all seven art families for publication, including usage rights. This is owner release approval, not evidence of an independent scholarly or tradition-institution review.

### Responsive delivery encodes — 15 October 2026

`collection-ramayana-560.avif`, `collection-ramayana-800.avif` and `collection-ramayana-1120.avif` are delivery-only encodes of the existing reviewed Ramayana WebP. They preserve its complete composition and aspect ratio, with no new painting, crop, sharpening, colour treatment or generated element. CSS retains the existing displayed crop. The original WebP files remain unchanged and serve as the unsupported-format fallback.

Source SHA-256: `efdc72236075b1bb71f70a105ce33f6a577857a3872802963c6e53d5d848a242`. The offline `generate-performance-assets.mjs` script refuses a different source and uses Sharp resize plus AVIF quality 55, effort 6, 4:4:4 chroma. Output dimensions are 560×700, 800×1000 and 1120×1400; sizes are 76,781, 127,864 and 241,597 bytes. The normal public build copies these precomputed files and needs no image-tool dependency. This transport optimization does not approve any pending narrative art or extend the illustrated-edition review scope.

The same transport-only process supplies `world-rama-{560,800,900}.avif` and `world-hanuman-{560,800,900}.avif` for the other eight public stories. Dimensions are 560×747, 800×1067 and 900×1200. Rama sizes: 56,687 / 87,507 / 108,569 bytes; Hanuman sizes: 51,600 / 80,817 / 100,459 bytes. `delivery-assets.mjs` pins the original source hashes and explicitly allowlists these three families. Original world files, their existing CSS crops and the pending narrative-art gate remain unchanged.

## Contextual reading worlds — 1 October 2026

Files: `world-rama.webp`, `world-krishna.webp`, `world-hanuman.webp`, `world-shiva.webp`

One original four-panel editorial panorama was generated with OpenAI `image_gen`, then cropped into four 900 × 1200 WebP reading-world assets. The brief deliberately avoided portraits, idols, text and claims of a canonical scene:

- Rama: a quiet forest path at sunrise, warm sandstone, river blue and a subtle bow-shaped horizon.
- Krishna: a midnight-indigo river, peacock teal, a lotus and restrained gold ripples.
- Hanuman: a wind-swept dawn landscape, sindoor earth, storm blue and an upward sunlit path.
- Shiva: Himalayan stillness, ash basalt, glacier blue and a fine copper crescent.

These images are atmospheric navigation art, not depictions of a deity or evidence about a sacred event. The owner's 1 October 2026 publication attestation covers these four contextual-world families and the three collection-art families above. It does not establish independent scholarly review or change the source facts. The original generation output is retained outside the website checkout in the task workspace.
